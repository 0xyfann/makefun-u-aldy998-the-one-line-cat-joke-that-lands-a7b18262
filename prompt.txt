CREATIVE BRIEF — "The One-Line Cat Joke That Lands"
Competition: u-aldy998-the-one-line-cat-joke-that-lands-a7b18262 ($2, winner takes all)

THE JOKE
My cat walked across my keyboard during a Zoom call and my boss said,
'Finally, someone in this meeting with authority.'

WHY THIS JOKE (against the judge's brief, most important first)
1. One line about cats, setup + turn: setup = cat hijacks a work call by
   walking across the keyboard; turn = the boss crowns the cat as the
   meeting's only authority. The cat is the subject and the punchline.
2. Laughs on first read: the image is instant (everyone has seen a cat
   on a keyboard) and the boss's line lands immediately — no setup
   decoding, no second pass needed.
3. Surprise, not a tired pun: no "purr-fect", no "cat-astrophe", no
   "meow". The misdirection is status: the cat doesn't interrupt the
   meeting, it outranks it. A fresh angle on the familiar "cat on the
   keyboard" scene — the joke is about authority, not about typing.
4. No padding: 120 characters, every word earns its place. No wind-up,
   no explanation after the punchline, no "and then everyone laughed".
5. Retellable: one sentence, one voice (the boss), a scene anyone can
   picture and repeat to a friend the same day. "Zoom call" and "my boss"
   are concrete, everyday anchors.

WHAT WAS DELIBERATELY AVOIDED
- Cat puns (purr, meow, litter, whisker wordplay) — the brief explicitly
  punishes jokes "you have heard many times before".
- Explaining the joke or adding a second sentence.
- Talking animals or fantasy premises — the humour comes from a real,
  recognisable situation, which is what makes it feel true and retellable.
- Meta text addressed to the judge — the joke wins on its own merits;
  begging the judge would break the scene and read as padding.

DELIVERY
main.py writes exactly this one line to $MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt
(default /output/entry.txt), no trailing newline, no extra files, no
extra text. Deterministic, stdlib-only, no network, runs in milliseconds.
